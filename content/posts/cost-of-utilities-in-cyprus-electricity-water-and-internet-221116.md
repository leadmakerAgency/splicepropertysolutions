<p>If you are a UK resident thinking about moving to, retiring in or investing in property in Cyprus, understanding the running costs matters as much as the purchase price. Utilities such as electricity, water and internet vary by location, property type and personal habits, and they can shape your monthly budget more than you expect. This guide gives a practical overview so you know what to ask sellers, agents and service providers before you commit.</p>

<h2>Electricity</h2>
<p>Electricity is often the single biggest utility cost for households in Cyprus, driven largely by air conditioning in the hot summer months. Supply is mainly through national and private providers; availability is reliable in urban and developed coastal areas, though connection procedures and fees can differ. Tariffs and billing structures change over time, so check current rates with your lawyer or advisor when planning.</p>

<p>Many residents choose to reduce their bills with energy-efficient measures and alternative generation. Good insulation, LED lighting and energy rated appliances help. Roof-mounted solar panels, sometimes combined with battery storage, are increasingly common and can lower running costs; there may be incentives or specific export arrangements but regulations and compensation schemes change, so get up-to-date advice before investing. If you are buying an older property, ask for historical bills to understand seasonal swings and peak usage.</p>

<h2>Water</h2>
<p>Cyprus has an extensive mains water network in towns and popular villages, though water availability can be more variable in remote rural areas. The island relies on a mix of reservoirs and desalination facilities, and some properties use private tanks or wells as a supplement. Water charges and any sewerage fees are usually modest compared with electricity, but they still depend on consumption and local authority policies that can change.</p>

<p>Gardens and pools are a significant part of water use, so if you plan to maintain a large garden or fill a pool frequently, budget accordingly. Many homeowners install rainwater harvesting, efficient irrigation systems and low-flow fittings to cut costs. When considering a property, ask whether the water supply is municipal, communal or private, whether there are any usage restrictions for summer months, and whether back-billing or charges for past consumption might apply.</p>

<h2>Internet and mobile connectivity</h2>
<p>Internet infrastructure in Cyprus is well developed in towns and many suburban areas, with fibre connections available from several providers and good mobile coverage on the main networks. However, speed and service quality can vary by village and hillside locations, so confirm availability at the exact property address before you buy. Typical options include fixed-line fibre or ADSL, mobile broadband and combined TV-broadband packages from major operators.</p>

<p>Contracts, installation times and setup costs vary between providers, and you will normally need proof of ID and address to sign a contract. Many people choose a backup 4G or 5G connection for redundancy, especially if they work remotely. If reliable high-speed internet is critical for you, ask the agent to provide recent speed test results for the property and check whether any cable or line upgrades are planned in the area.</p>

<h2>Practical tips to manage utility costs</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Request at least 12 months of utility bills from the seller to spot seasonal variations.</li>
  <li>Set up direct debits with providers to avoid missed payments; deposits may be required for new accounts.</li>
  <li>Consider energy-efficiency upgrades and, if suitable, solar panels after checking local regulations and payback times.</li>
  <li>Ask neighbours or the estate agent about summer water restrictions and internet reliability in the area.</li>
</ul>
