<p>If you are thinking to retire in Cyprus, you are considering a Mediterranean island with warm winters, a relaxed pace of life and a range of locations from busy Limassol to quieter Paphos and Larnaca. Many UK retirees choose Cyprus for the climate, English spoken widely and easy flight connections. This guide explains how moving, buying property and settling in typically work, and what you should check before making decisions.</p>

<h2>How to retire in Cyprus: residency, visas and paperwork</h2>
<p>Residency rules vary depending on whether you are an EU citizen or a third country national. UK citizens usually need to apply for the appropriate residence permit, often a temporary or long term permit for financially independent persons, work, or family reasons.</p>
<p>Expect to provide proof of income or savings, accommodation, health insurance and a clean criminal record. Application processes can take several weeks and requirements change, so you should confirm current rules with the Cyprus Civil Registry and Migration Department or through a lawyer.</p>

<h2>Where to live: towns and lifestyle choices</h2>
<p>Choose location based on lifestyle. Limassol is cosmopolitan, with luxury developments, restaurants and an international business scene, while Paphos has a long established British community and a gentler pace suited to retirees.</p>
<p>Larnaca offers good international flight links and a quieter urban feel, ideal if you value convenience without big city bustle. Ayia Napa and Protaras are famous for beaches and lively summer seasons, which may suit those wanting a holiday vibe, whereas Nicosia is inland and more administrative in character.</p>

<h2>Buying property in Cyprus: process and practical tips</h2>
<p>Non residents can buy property in Cyprus, though the purchase process is different from the UK. Typically you will appoint a local lawyer to carry out title searches, check planning permissions and negotiate contracts before paying a deposit and completing at a notary.</p>
