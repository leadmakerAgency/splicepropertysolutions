<p>If you are a UK investor, retiree or someone thinking of moving part of your business to the island, understanding the cyprus corporate tax rate is a sensible first step. Cyprus has long attracted international businesses because of a relatively low headline rate, a broad treaty network and practical English language services in cities such as Limassol and Nicosia. This guide explains how the system generally works, what it means for UK-based people, and the practical steps you may need to take before committing.</p>

<h2>Understanding the cyprus corporate tax rate</h2>
<p>The headline corporate tax rate in Cyprus is widely known as one of the more competitive in Europe. That rate typically applies to taxable profits of companies resident in Cyprus, subject to the normal rules on deductions and allowable expenses. While the headline figure is important, remember that your effective tax position can be shaped by allowances, transfer pricing rules, and other local reliefs.</p>

<h2>Who is taxed in Cyprus and how residency works</h2>
<p>Companies are generally taxed in Cyprus if they are tax resident there or if their profits arise from activities in Cyprus. Tax residency often hinges on where a company’s central management and control are exercised. For individuals, tax residency rules determine whether you pay Cyprus tax on worldwide income or just local source income, and these rules can be different from those in the UK.</p>
<p>If you are considering relocating to Cyprus, places like Paphos and Larnaca are popular for retirees, while Limassol attracts international business owners and executives. Your personal tax residency status may affect how dividends and salaries are taxed, so it is worth checking how many days you can spend in the UK and Cyprus before residency tests apply.</p>
