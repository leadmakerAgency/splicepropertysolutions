<h2>Why Cyprus appeals to divers and property seekers</h2>
<p>If you are considering moving to Cyprus, investing here, or retiring by the sea, diving in Cyprus is a compelling reason to consider the island. The Mediterranean waters are clear, the coastline is varied, and dive centres operate year round, making it easy to combine everyday life with regular underwater adventures. Many British residents appreciate the short flights from the UK, familiar services and an island lifestyle that supports both leisure diving and a quieter retirement pace.</p>

<h2>Diving in Cyprus: top sites and what to expect</h2>
<p>Cyprus offers a mix of dramatic wrecks, sculpted reefs and sheltered bays. The Zenobia wreck off Larnaca is internationally famous and often cited as a must-do for experienced divers. Around Paphos and Limassol you will find reefs, caverns and smaller wrecks suited to a range of abilities. Ayia Napa and Protaras, especially Cape Greco and the Blue Lagoon, are popular for boat dives, sea caves and excellent visibility.</p>
<p>Visibility is typically good, often excellent in summer, and many sites are accessible from shore as well as by boat. Marine life includes colourful reef fish, groupers, octopus and moray eels, along with seasonal visitors such as turtles in the Akamas and Lara Bay areas. Each region has its character, so you can choose properties close to your preferred dive style, from sheltered bays to deeper wreck sites.</p>

<h2>Practical seasonality, water conditions and certification</h2>
<p>Diving conditions vary through the year. The main season runs from spring through autumn when water temperatures are warmer and visibility peaks, though many operators still run dives in winter for robust divers. Water temperatures can feel noticeably cooler in winter, so a thicker wetsuit or drysuit may be necessary depending on comfort levels.</p>
<p>Your existing certifications from PADI, BSAC or other recognised agencies will generally be accepted on the island. Advanced and wreck-specific dives, like Zenobia, often require additional experience or qualifications, and dive centres may ask for logged dives as proof of experience. You will typically need a current dive medical if you plan to enrol on a course or do more technical dives.</p>

<h2>Courses, gear and local dive centres</h2>
<p>Most coastal towns have multiple dive centres offering everything from try-dives to instructor training. Larnaca, Limassol, Paphos, Ayia Napa and Protaras are well served. If you are moving here, you can decide to bring your own kit or buy locally. Bringing a personal regulator, mask and computer can be worthwhile, while heavy items like tanks and cylinders are usually hired locally.</p>
<p>Costs and course availability vary by centre, season and demand. Beginners often start with an open water course which can be completed over several days, while ongoing training such as specialties and instructor courses are available for those who want to make diving a bigger part of daily life in Cyprus. Always check medical, age and insurance requirements with the dive operator in advance.</p>
