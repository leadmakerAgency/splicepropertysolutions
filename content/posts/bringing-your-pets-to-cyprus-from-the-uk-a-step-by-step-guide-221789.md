<p>If you are a UK resident thinking about moving, retiring or investing in property in Cyprus, bringing your pet with you is usually possible with planning and the right paperwork. Rules changed after Brexit, so the process is different from the old pet passport system. This guide sets out the practical steps to prepare your dog, cat or ferret for travel, what to expect on the day and how to make sure you meet Cyprus import requirements.</p>

<h2>Before you book travel</h2>
<p>Start well in advance. The key legal requirements typically include a microchip, up to date rabies vaccination, and an official health certificate issued by an authorised UK veterinarian shortly before travel. Some animals may also require additional tests or treatments, especially for dogs, so check with your vet and with Cyprus authorities. If your pet’s microchip is not the international standard, discuss options with your vet so it can be read on arrival.</p>

<p>It helps to work through a clear checklist. At a minimum, make sure you have:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Microchip implantation and documentation</li>
  <li>Rabies vaccination record</li>
  <li>Animal Health Certificate or equivalent issued by an authorised veterinarian</li>
  <li>Any required parasite treatment for entry</li>
  <li>Copies of veterinary records and your contact details</li>
</ul>

<h2>Booking travel and preparing your pet</h2>
<p>Different airlines, ferries and routes have their own rules about transporting animals. Some carriers allow pets in the cabin, others only in the hold or as cargo. Choose a reputable carrier with experience handling pets and confirm their crate, size and booking requirements early. If you plan to fly, book a direct flight where possible to reduce stress and the chance of missed connections.</p>

<p>Invest time in crate training so your pet is comfortable being in its travel container. Familiar bedding and a favourite toy can help. Consider a vet visit to discuss travel-related stress, motion sickness and whether a short course of medication is appropriate. Never sedate an animal for air travel without professional veterinary advice.</p>

<h2>Arrival procedures in Cyprus</h2>
<p>On arrival, your pet will be inspected by Cyprus veterinary or customs officials. Make sure you have original documents and copies to present, including the health certificate and vaccination records. Failure to meet entry requirements can result in quarantine, return to the UK or other measures, so double-check every step before departure.</p>

<p>Once through formalities, register with a local vet in Cyprus soon after arrival. They will advise on local healthcare, parasite prevention suited to Mediterranean conditions and any follow-up vaccinations or health checks required by local law. You may also need to notify local authorities or your property’s management depending on where you will be living.</p>

<h2>Practical tips and where to get help</h2>
<p>Keep digital and paper copies of all documents and have them easily accessible during travel. If your move involves family members travelling separately, ensure someone is designated to carry the original certificates. Research pet-friendly accommodation and the local environment so you can help your pet adjust to the climate and lifestyle in Cyprus.</p>

<p>Rules and required timings can change, and some details depend on which part of the UK you are travelling from. Always confirm requirements with your vet, with the UK government guidance on pet travel and with Cyprus consular or veterinary authorities. For complex cases, such as older animals, exotic pets or commercial moves, consider a specialist pet relocation service or legal advice.</p>
