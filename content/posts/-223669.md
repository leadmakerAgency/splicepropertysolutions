<h2>Welcome and first steps</h2>
<p>If you are relocating to cyprus from the UK, you are joining a place with long summers, varied coastlines and a network of expat-friendly towns. Start by clarifying why you want to move: work, retirement, part-time living or property investment. That clarity will guide visa choices, where to live and what paperwork you will need.</p>

<h2>Relocating to Cyprus: residency, visas and permissions</h2>
<p>Post-Brexit, British citizens are generally treated as third country nationals for long stays, so you may need a residence permit rather than relying on free movement. EU citizens have different, usually simpler, rights to live and work. For most people the options include short-term visas, long-stay permits linked to employment, family reunification, or investment and pensioner categories.</p>
<p>Immigration requirements, timelines and documentation vary by individual and change over time, so always check with the Cyprus Civil Registry and Migration Department or an immigration lawyer. You may also need a national identity number, register with the local municipality and apply for a tax identification number once you plan to stay long term.</p>

<h2>Choosing where to live: towns and what they offer</h2>
<p>Cyprus is compact, but locations feel distinct. Limassol is cosmopolitan, with international business, marina developments and year-round services. Paphos is popular with retirees, offering a relaxed pace and easy access to beaches and cultural sites. Larnaca and Nicosia are practical for schools, hospitals and government services, while Ayia Napa and Protaras are seasonal hotspots for holiday rentals.</p>
<p>Consider commute times, healthcare access and community when choosing. If you plan to rent first, you can sample different areas before buying. Demand, prices and rental seasonality vary between towns, so match your lifestyle and investment aims to the locality.</p>

<h2>Buying property in Cyprus: the practical process</h2>
<p>Buying property often starts with a reservation agreement and deposit, then moves to a contract of sale prepared by a lawyer. You will typically instruct a Cypriot solicitor to carry out title searches, ensure planning permissions are valid, and manage stamp duty and transfer fees. Title deeds can sometimes take months or longer to be issued, so expect some administrative lead time.</p>
<p>New builds may be subject to VAT, while resale homes have different costs. You should budget for conveyancing fees, VAT where applicable, transfer taxes and any community or municipal charges. Always have an independent survey and a solicitor who speaks English and knows local property law.</p>
