<h2>Overview: what you should know about cyprus new build apartments</h2>
<p>Buying a new build in Cyprus can feel attractive, whether you plan to move, retire, or invest from the UK. Cyprus new build apartments are often marketed with modern fixtures, energy efficient designs, and amenities such as pools and secure parking, and you will commonly find them in coastal centres such as Limassol, Paphos, Larnaca, Ayia Napa and Protaras. Before you commit, learn the key steps, where costs appear, and the practical checks you should make from overseas.</p>

<h2>The typical buying process and paperwork</h2>
<p>New builds are usually sold off plan or near completion, and the route to ownership starts with a reservation agreement followed by a formal contract for sale. Your solicitor will check planning permits, building permit and whether the developer holds clear title to the land or has title deeds available for transfer at completion. Expect staged payments linked to construction milestones rather than a single transaction.</p>
<p>Completion normally requires an energy performance certificate and a final occupancy or completion certificate from the local authority. Title deeds in Cyprus can sometimes be delayed, so your lawyer should explain how the transfer will happen, what security exists for your deposit, and any guarantees the developer offers.</p>

<h2>Costs to budget for beyond the purchase price</h2>
<p>The headline price for a new apartment is only part of the cost. You will typically pay legal fees, stamp duty and registration costs, potential VAT on the first sale from the developer, and any mortgage-related fees. After purchase, budget for annual service charges, municipal rates, utility connections and insurance.</p>
<p>If you plan to rent, factor in property management fees and routine maintenance. These recurring costs vary with the building and location, for example higher communal service charges are common in luxury seafront developments in Limassol than in smaller inland complexes near Paphos.</p>

<h2>Financing from the UK: mortgages and practicalities</h2>
<p>Some Cypriot banks offer mortgages to foreign buyers, and a number of international lenders provide cross-border mortgage products aimed at British nationals. Lenders will assess income, credit history and the property itself, and borrowing terms for overseas buyers can differ from local residents. Getting a decision in principle before committing is a sensible step, especially for off-plan purchases.</p>
