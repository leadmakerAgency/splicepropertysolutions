<p>If you are a UK buyer thinking about moving, retiring or investing in property in Cyprus, understanding transfer fees is essential before you sign anything. Transfer fees are part of the cost of getting a property legally registered in your name, and the rules and practicalities differ from the UK. Knowing who normally pays what, how fees are calculated and which other costs to expect will help you budget and avoid surprises during completion.</p>

<h2>What are transfer fees and who pays them?</h2>
<p>Transfer fees are the charges applied by the Land Registry when ownership of a property is formally transferred. In Cyprus these are separate from legal fees, stamp duty and any tax liabilities. Who pays transfer fees can often be agreed between buyer and seller, but in practice buyers commonly bear at least some of the cost. For new properties bought from a developer there may be a different arrangement, and in some cases developers cover the transfer fees as part of their marketing package. Always check the sale contract so you know who is contractually responsible.</p>

<h2>How fees are calculated and common variations</h2>
<p>The method used to calculate transfer fees varies. They can be based on the contract price, the market value, or on a previous recorded value related to the seller. Because legislation and Land Registry practice change from time to time, the actual basis and scale of the fee will depend on when the property was first registered, how it is being transferred and whether VAT applies. VAT may be payable on new builds, in which case the treatment of transfer fees differs from a resale. For this reason it is essential to confirm calculations with your lawyer or the Land Registry before completing the purchase.</p>

<h2>Practical steps and other costs to expect</h2>
<p>Paying the transfer fee is just one part of the registration process. Your solicitor will usually handle the application to the District Land Office, ensure all documents are in order and pay the fee on registration day. There can be additional administrative items that delay registration if not sorted in advance, such as outstanding municipal charges, utility bills or unresolved encumbrances on the title. You should also factor in legal fees, notary costs if needed, stamp duty on the contract and any other taxes that might apply. Timing matters because the transfer cannot be completed until the Land Registry has accepted the documentation and the fee has been settled.</p>

<h2>Negotiation, due diligence and professional advice</h2>
<p>Many of the misunderstandings around transfer fees are avoided by clear negotiation and thorough due diligence. Confirm in writing who pays what, ask your lawyer to obtain a pre-transfer ledger search to reveal any debts or charges, and check whether the property is being sold subject to VAT. Because fee scales and legal rules change, get up-to-date advice from a Cyprus-qualified lawyer and a tax adviser if necessary. If you are transferring funds from the UK, also consider currency exchange timing and instructions so money is available at completion.</p>
